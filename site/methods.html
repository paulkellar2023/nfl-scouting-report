How it works

Every number on this site comes from open data and a fixed, published method. Nothing is hand-picked. This page explains what each metric means, how the data is cleaned and how the cards, keys and win probabilities are chosen.

Data

Play-by-play, schedules and betting lines, rosters, injury reports, snap counts and FTN charting (play action, motion, blitzers, pass rushers, interception-worthy throws), all from nflverse through the nflreadpy package. Also from nflverse: NFL Next Gen Stats (time to throw, tight-window throws, rush yards over expected, separation and cushion, stacked boxes) and Pro Football Reference charting (pressures, broken and missed tackles), expected fantasy points and weekly player stats from nflverse, and ESPN QBR. Our Elo ratings use only final scores since 2015. The site rebuilds Thursday at 2 PM ET (full report and the Thursday line, after the week's injury reports have had time to come in), then again Sunday at 11 AM, 3 PM and 7 PM ET and Monday at 6 PM ET (newer injury statuses and lines for the model). Once a game kicks off, its report is frozen.

Cleaning

  • Plays: regular-season passes and runs only. No kneels, spikes, penalty-only plays or special teams. Sacks and scrambles count as dropbacks.
  • Garbage time removed: efficiency stats use plays with a win probability between 10% and 90%.
  • Neutral script: tendency stats (how often a team throws) use a stricter filter: win probability 20% to 80%, a one-score game, and not the last 2 minutes of a half.
  • Recency: each week's plays are weighted with a 6-week half-life, so recent form counts more.

Metrics

EPA (expected points added)
The change in expected points from before a play to after it, based on down, distance, field position and time. +0.10 EPA per play is good, 0 is average.
Success rate
Share of plays with positive EPA.
Explosive play rate
Share of plays that are dropbacks of 20+ yards or designed runs of 10+ yards.
Pass rate over expected (PROE)
Actual pass rate minus the nflverse expected pass rate for the same situations. Positive = throws more than a typical team would.
Completion % over expected (CPOE)
Completion rate minus the expected rate given throw depth and situation (nflverse model).
aDOT
Average depth of target: air yards per pass attempt. "Air yards vs. normal" for a defense compares opponents' aDOT against their own season average.
YAC over expected
Yards after catch minus the nflverse expected YAC for that catch.
Stuff rate
Share of designed runs that gain 0 yards or less.
Pressure rate
Share of dropbacks with pressure. When charted pressure data is not available for a season, the site uses QB hits plus sacks instead and labels it "QB hit + sack rate".
Shrunk to league mean
Small samples are pulled toward the league average in proportion to their noise (empirical Bayes, method of moments). A team with 40 plays moves more than a team with 400.
Brier score
Mean squared error of win probabilities. Lower is better. Always guessing 50% scores 0.250.
Factor contributions (SHAP)
How many points each model input pushes the projected margin. The model is linear, so these values are exact and sum to the projection.
Playoff leverage
How much a game moves each team's playoff odds: P(playoffs if win) minus P(playoffs if loss), summed over both teams.

Opponent adjustment

Pass and rush EPA are opponent-adjusted with a play-level ridge regression: each play's EPA = offense effect + defense effect + home field. The ridge penalty equals the noise variance over the signal variance, so the regression is also the shrinkage. Passing ratings use only the projected starting QB's dropbacks.

Cards & keys

Each card template pairs an offensive measure with the same measure allowed by defenses. The edge for a matchup is z(offense) + z(opposing defense), where each z is the shrunk value over the raw league spread, so noisy stats score lower. Tendency cards (play action, blitz, man coverage) scale the edge by how often the choosing side uses it. Run-gap cards pick the best of 7 gaps, so they pay a fixed penalty for that extra choice. The top cards are shown, with at most 2 per phase. The 3 keys to success per team come from the largest edges involving that team plus the team's own biggest strengths and weaknesses this season (a stat at least 1 standard deviation from the league average). Each team gets at least one key of its own when one qualifies, at most one key per phase, and a stat is used only once per game, so the two teams never get the mirror image of the same matchup. "EDGE" means the key favors the team, "WATCH" means it does not.

How many cards: a default view shows 6 cards whenever 6 exist (fewer only when fewer qualify, and then 4, never an odd number). Cards above the relevance floor go first. Open slots go to the best remaining cards, with a strong preference for card types the week's other games are not showing. The Team tab has at most 14 cards in all.

Variety across games: a card type already in the default view of earlier games this week loses up to 15% of its score, in proportion to how many of those games showed it. The cut is small, so it only changes the order when two cards are close; a clearly stronger card still wins. Games are built in schedule order, so earlier games are least affected.

CARD TYPES filter: the chips above the cards (Field charts, Passing, Rushing, Pressure, Tendencies, Special teams & 4th, Form & luck; on the Players tab, Field charts, Quarterbacks, Rushing, Receiving) start on the types in the ranked default view. Turning a type on adds every card of that type. A "+N" chip shows hidden cards in a type that is already on. RECOMMENDED goes back to the ranked view.

The "Who has the edge" summary is written by an LLM (Claude) from the keys, the top cards, the players to watch and the model and market numbers only. It has three sentences: the matchup most likely to decide the game, a player angle, and our number against the market. Each summary is checked before it is shown: every number must appear in those facts, it must be three sentences, and it must not reuse the rule-based summary's stock phrases. A reply that fails gets one retry; if that also fails, the rule-based summary is shown and the label says so.

Win-probability model

A ridge regression on the home team's margin, converted to a win probability with a normal distribution: P(win) = Φ(margin / σ). Inputs (home minus away): passing matchup, rushing matchup, starting QB value (EPA per dropback, shrunk toward replacement level), offensive and defensive injury load (players listed Out, Doubtful or Questionable, weighted by recent snap share), rest, and home field. All inputs use only games before the one being predicted. The ridge penalty is picked by leave-one-season-out validation and σ by log loss. The track record is a walk-forward backtest since 2018: each season is predicted by a model trained only on earlier seasons and scored against the closing line.

Home-page tiles show the favorite and its win probability (for example "CIN 54%"), or EVEN at 50%. Each matchup shows three numbers: MODEL, MARKET and our ELO.

It is a different lens on the data, not betting advice. The market usually beats it.

Luck adjustments in the model

  • Record luck does not enter the model. It rates teams on play-by-play efficiency, not wins or points, so close-game luck (the LUCK column on the league table) never moves its odds. Playoff seeds still use real records, because that is how the NFL seeds.
  • Fumble-recovery luck: who falls on a loose ball is close to a coin flip. A second version of the ratings gives every fumble play the season's average result for a fumble (pass or run) instead of what happened. Each build fits both versions and keeps the one with the lower leave-one-season-out log loss, so this is only used when it predicts better.
  • Injury luck: "Injuries behind ratings" is the recency-weighted injury load (players listed Out, Doubtful or Questionable, weighted by snap share) each team carried in the games its ratings come from, home minus away. The model learns from past seasons how much a team that played hurt tends to beat its ratings. Future weeks and playoff games are then projected at full health (no current injury load).
  • Limits: players on injured reserve leave the weekly report, so long-term injuries count only while listed, and a projection at full health is wrong for a player who is out for the season. Interception luck is not adjusted yet: the charting that flags interception-worthy throws only goes back to 2022.

Track record

Every finished game is graded with the Brier score and log loss, for the model and for the market (vig-free moneyline, else the spread), on the same games. Ties are left out.

  • Published picks: the last version of each game shown on this site before kickoff, graded against the market number from that same build. This is the honest live record.
  • Season walk-forward: every game this season, where week N is predicted by a model fit only on games before week N. It uses the QB who actually started, so it is slightly optimistic, and the market number is the closing line.
  • Past seasons: the same walk-forward backtest by season, plus a calibration chart (predicted vs. actual win rate in 10-point bins).

About 270 games per season is a small sample. A Brier gap smaller than about 0.01 is mostly noise.

Backfilled weeks

Weeks built after the season started (marked "backfilled") use only data from before that week, the same as a live build, but the line is the closing line and the injury report is the final one for that week. They get recaps like any other week. On the track record page they have their own tile, separate from published picks (which only counts picks shown on the site before kickoff), and they are marked BACKFILL in the picks table.

Next Gen Stats and PFR cards

Weekly Next Gen Stats and PFR rows are combined with the same 6-week recency weights as the play-by-play stats. Player stats need a minimum sample (100 attempts for QBs, 50 carries for RBs, 25 targets for receivers) and are not shrunk. Team stats (stacked boxes, pressure, tackling) are shrunk to the league mean. When PFR pressures are available they replace the "QB hit + sack" proxy for the team pressure card; the play-level proxy is still used for the clean-pocket and four-man-rush cards and for recaps. None of these are opponent-adjusted.

Special teams and fourth downs

  • Special teams EPA per game: expected points added on punts, kickoffs, field goals and extra points, credited to the kicking or receiving side, recency-weighted and shrunk to the league mean.
  • Field goal points over expected: 3 × (field goals made minus expected makes), where the expected make rate comes from nflverse's field goal model when present, otherwise from a logistic fit on kick distance. Not shrunk; kickers need 5+ attempts. Kicker cards rank lower than the other special teams cards, and at most 3 games per week show one in the default view.
  • Fourth-down decisions (4th down bot): when Ben Baldwin's nfl4th model is available, the 4th-down card uses it. For every 4th down, nfl4th estimates how much win probability going for it adds compared with the best kick ("go boost"). The card shows how often a team went for it when going for it added at least 1 win-probability point (recency-weighted, shrunk to the league mean), and how many win-probability points per game its 4th-down calls gave up compared with the bot's call. When it is available, it ranks ahead of the 4th-down map: if both would be in a game's default Team view, the nfl4th card takes the map's slot (unless the map is the only field chart; the map stays under the CARD TYPES filter). At least one game each week shows the nfl4th card: if none ranks it into the default view, it goes into the game where it scores highest. If the nfl4th file is missing or has no plays for this season yet, the card falls back to the rule below and is ranked like any other card.
  • Fourth-down aggressiveness (fallback): share of 4th-and-1 or 4th-and-2 at or past the team's own 40 where the offense runs a play instead of punting or kicking, with win probability 10-90% and outside the last 2 minutes of each half. Shrunk to the league mean.

Play-type tendencies

  • Target zones: each throw is placed in one of 6 zones: short (under 10 air yards) or deep, and left, middle or right from the quarterback's view. The card shows where an offense throws most often compared with the league, next to the EPA per throw the opposing defense allows in each zone (shrunk to the league mean). It picks the zone the offense uses unusually often where the defense is unusually bad, or unusually good.
  • Top receiver defense: each offense's top receiver is the player with the most recency-weighted targets (any position). For each defense, the card measures EPA per target above or below each receiver's own season average, so a defense that faced elite receivers is not punished for it, separately for opposing top receivers and for all other targets (shrunk to the league mean, garbage time removed). The chart is a dumbbell: filled dot = vs. top receivers, open dot = vs. everyone else. The player named in the card must have been targeted in his team's last 2 games, a simple guard against naming an injured, inactive or traded player; the injury report itself is not used. The card appears when the defense is unusually stingy or generous with top receivers.
  • Pass or run lean: the offense's pass rate over expected in neutral situations vs. how much worse the defense is against the pass than the run (opponent-adjusted EPA allowed, in z-scores). The card says whether the offense's habit plays into the defense's weakness or its strength.

Visual guide

Every card has one headline, one chart and a source line. The chart always shows the whole league or the whole season, with the two teams in this game highlighted in their matchup colors. The examples below show the card format only: the teams, players and numbers are made up.

Comparison charts (most Team and Players cards)

Duel
Shows: one offense measure against the same measure allowed by the opposing defense, each placed on the league range. Read it: the label shows which end is better. When the offense and the defense both sit at the end that favors the offense, the offense has a clear edge. "Bengals average +0.18 EPA per dropback, 4th. Steelers allow +0.09, 25th."
League bars
Shows: all 32 teams ranked on one stat, with the league median as a dashed line. The value for each team in this game sits in a box over its bar. Used for special teams EPA, field goals over expected, fourth-down aggressiveness and fantasy points allowed. "Ravens special teams add +2.1 EPA per game, 2nd."
Team scatter
Shows: all 32 teams on two axes, usually how often a team does something (x) against how well it works (y), such as blitz rate vs. EPA allowed when blitzing. Read it: the dashed lines are league medians, so each corner is a type of team.
Strips
Shows: each team as a dot on one line per row, for a habit on one row and the opponent's weakness on the next (the pass-or-run lean card). Read it: dots far from center are unusual. "Lions lean on the run (-6% pass rate over expected), 3rd-most run-heavy."
Target zones
Shows: 6 zones (short or deep, left, middle or right). On the left is the share of an offense's throws in each zone. On the right is the EPA per throw the defense allows there. The zone the card talks about is outlined.
Run gaps
Shows: EPA per run through each of 7 gaps, from left end to right end, offense vs. defense allowed.
Form trend
Shows: each team's net EPA per play by week. Use it to see whether a team is getting better or worse, not how good it is overall.
Player strip
Shows: every qualifying player on one line (QB EPA per dropback, YAC over expected, QBR). The players in this game are labeled.
Player scatter
Shows: qualifying players on two axes, such as target share vs. EPA per target. On the fantasy tab there is a diagonal line where actual = expected: players above it scored more than their usage suggests.
Player bars
Shows: bars that go left or right from zero, one per player in this game: fantasy points over expected, touchdowns over expected, or change in snap share. "Chase is +4.2 PPR points per game over expected, the biggest gap in this game."

Field charts

Field charts draw plays on a football field instead of on an axis. Each matchup shows at least one and at most two on the Team tab, and on the Players tab when one qualifies. When only 4 regular cards qualify, the two best field charts are added after them.

Pass chart
Every pass by the offense, placed by air yards (exact) and left, middle or right. Play-by-play has no exact sideline spot, so dots are spread inside each lane. Next to it is the opposing defense's completion % allowed in 12 zones vs. the league. "Bengals throw deep to the right on 14% of passes."
Run chart
Every designed run by gap and yards gained, next to the defense's yards per carry allowed in each gap. "Ravens run behind the left guard on 22% of designed runs."
Drive chart
Every drive from each team's last 2 games, from start to end, colored by result. "Bills scored 2.9 points per drive over their last 2 games."
Fourth-down map
Every 4th down by field position and yards to go. The shading is the league's go-for-it rate in each spot, so a "go" dot in a pale area was an aggressive call. "Eagles went for it 11 times on 4th down, about 4 more than a typical team would."
Red-zone map
Every play snapped inside the 20, and touchdown rate allowed by distance. "Chiefs score touchdowns on 68% of red-zone trips, 3rd-highest."
Down-and-distance grid
Success rate by down and distance for the offense, next to the defense. Cells with fewer than 8 plays are left blank. "On 3rd and short, Browns succeed on 41% of plays (league 55%)."
Explosive-play map
Every play of 20+ yards as an arrow from snap to finish. "Dolphins average 4.1 plays of 20+ yards per game, 2nd."
Win-probability timeline
Each team's win probability through its last 2 games, plus blown leads and comebacks this season. "Jets have blown 3 games they led with 80%+ win odds this season."
Scoring by quarter
Average points scored and allowed in each quarter. "49ers outscore opponents by +4.8 points per game in the 3rd quarter."
Field-position tug-of-war
Where each team starts its drives on average, and where its opponents start. "Vikings start drives at their own 33 on average; their opponents start at their 25."
Third-down chart
Every 3rd down by yards to go, made or missed: the offense on top, the opposing defense below. "Commanders convert 47% of 3rd downs, 4th-highest."
Penalty breakdown
Flags per game by type for each team. "Raiders are flagged 7.4 times per game, 1st."
Play-action and motion splits
EPA per dropback with and without play action or pre-snap motion, and how often each team uses it (FTN charting).
Receiver chart (Players)
Every target to a team's top receiver by depth and side, colored by result. "Jefferson catches 71% of his targets, 11.2 yards downfield on average."
Pressure map (Players)
Each quarterback's EPA per dropback under pressure against his average time to throw, or pressure rate faced when Next Gen Stats are missing.
Target tree (Players)
A team's top 5 targets by depth, share and EPA per target.

Model charts (Model tab)

MODEL · MARKET · ELO bars
Three win probabilities for the same game: our model, the vig-free market and our Elo. When the three disagree, look at the waterfall to see why.
Factor waterfall
How many points each input moves the projected margin (SHAP). The bars add up to the total under the line, PROJECTED MARGIN.
Margin distribution
The range of likely final margins from the model. Each side of zero is shaded in the color of the team that wins there, and the larger area is the favorite.
Units table
Pass offense, rush offense, pass defense and rush defense for both teams, as opponent-adjusted EPA per play vs. league average (right = better). These are the model's inputs, so you can see which unit drives the rating. Starting QBs are listed below.
Elo trend
Each team's Elo rating game by game.
Injuries
Players listed Out, Doubtful or Questionable, by snap share. This card is hidden when neither team lists anyone.

Other pages use the same chart styles: cumulative Brier score and a calibration chart on the track record page, and a weekly line chart on team pages.

Matchup colors

The home team always keeps its main color. If the away team's color looks too much like it, the away team switches to its first alternate color that does not. Two colors count as too similar if they are close in RGB, or if they are in the same hue family (less than 35° apart on the color wheel) and both are saturated. This keeps gold vs. yellow (Ravens vs. Chargers) and orange vs. orange (Bengals vs. Browns) apart. If no alternate color works, the away team is shown in light gray. Some teams' alternates are duller than their real colors.

Elo

Our own Elo ratings, built only from final scores since 2015 (538-style): K = 20, home field worth 48 Elo points, a margin-of-victory multiplier that is damped for heavy favorites, and one third of each rating pulled back to the average (1505) every offseason. Elo ignores how teams play, so it is an independent check on the model. It is shown next to the model and market and graded on the track record page.

Fantasy tab

Up to six cards per game from nflverse's expected fantasy points model (PPR), weekly player stats and ESPN QBR, ranked by how unusual each finding is: points per game over or under expected, touchdowns over or under expected, expected points per game (opportunity), points allowed by the opposing defense to each position, WOPR (1.5 × target share + 0.7 × air-yard share), QBR, and snap-share trends (offensive snap share in a player's last 3 games minus his earlier games, from PFR snap counts). Expected points come from each player's targets, carries, air yards and field position, so a big gap between actual and expected usually shrinks. Season-to-date averages, not recency-weighted, and points allowed are not adjusted for the offenses a defense faced.

Post-game recaps

After a game, each key to success is checked against the same stat in that game, using plays with a win probability between 10% and 90%. The offense "wins" a key if its in-game value beats the league average for that stat (for pressure and stuff rate, if it is lower). A team wins the key if its side won it. Keys need a minimum number of plays (for example 8 dropbacks for passing EPA); below that they are marked "too few plays". Run-gap, coverage-shell, form and luck keys are not graded. Recaps are added on the next build after nflverse publishes the game's play-by-play, usually the next morning.

League table & team pages

NET = opponent-adjusted offensive EPA per play minus opponent-adjusted defensive EPA per play allowed (all plays, garbage time removed, recency-weighted). Pass and rush ranks use the same adjustment as the cards. TREND compares today's NET rank with the NET rank from plays through 4 weeks earlier. PROJ is the average final record across the 10,000 season simulations. LUCK = actual wins minus Pythagorean expected wins, where expected win % = PF2.37 / (PF2.37 + PA2.37). The weekly chart on team pages shows raw game values, so it does not remove opponent strength.

End-of-season outcomes (team pages): the same 10,000 simulations of the remaining regular season are extended through the playoffs. Seeds come from each simulated final record (division winners 1-4, then three wild cards; tiebreakers approximate). Each playoff game is decided with the same ratings as the regular-season simulations: the higher seed is at home, seed 1 has a bye, the bracket reseeds after the wild card round, and the Super Bowl is neutral. The chart splits the team's simulated seasons by the round where the season ends ("Out in divisional" = reached the divisional round and lost there; a bye team's first game is in the divisional round), so the shares add up to 100%. The subtitle gives the odds of reaching each round, which add the later rounds together and, for seasons that miss the playoffs, the draft slot: the 18 non-playoff teams pick 1-18 by fewest wins, with ties broken at random (the NFL uses strength of schedule). Shares under 1% show as "<1%". These ratings are not the luck-adjusted game model, so a team's title odds here can differ a little from the bracket, which fills each round with the model's favorite.

Projected weeks

Every remaining regular-season game through week 18 gets the full set of cards, built from the same data as this week (so the matchup stats are what each team has done so far). The model uses today's ratings and projected starters, no injury report, and the scheduled rest. The summary is rule-based. Projections are rebuilt on every run, so they improve as the season goes on.

Playoff bracket

Until the regular season ends, seeds come from projected final records: each team's average final win total across 10,000 simulations of the remaining games (real results so far plus the model's ratings for the rest). Division winners are seeds 1-4, then three wild cards. Projected ties are rare; when they happen, and once the season is over and real standings take over, ties are broken by head-to-head (within the tied group), division record (same division only), conference record, point differential, then team name. This is approximate: the NFL's full list has more steps, such as common games and strength of victory. Each round is filled by the model favorite; the higher seed hosts and the Super Bowl is neutral. After week 18, real matchups and results from the schedule replace projections as they are set.

Playoff leverage & standings

The rest of the season is simulated 10,000 times. This week's games use the market spread, and later games use the model's ratings, regressed toward even. Seeding is approximate: division winners, then wild cards by wins, with division wins and then a coin flip as tiebreakers.

Weather

Games in the next 15 days show a kickoff forecast from Open-Meteo: an icon on the game tile and the temperature at the top right of the matchup page. Wind (15 mph or more) and the chance of rain or snow (40% or more) are added when they matter; hover or tap for the full forecast. Domes and stadiums whose roof is usually closed show "Indoors". The forecast is the hour closest to kickoff and updates with each build, then freezes at kickoff with the rest of the report. Weather does not enter the model.

Limits

  • Mid-season samples are small. Shrinkage helps, but single-game swings still matter.
  • Charted data (coverage shells, true pressure) is not always available for the current season. Cards that need it are skipped.
  • Injury reports change after Friday. Inactives announced 90 minutes before kickoff are not included.
