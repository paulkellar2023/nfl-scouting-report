How it works

Every number on this site comes from open data and a fixed, published method. Nothing is hand-picked. This page explains what each metric means, how the data is cleaned and how the cards, keys and win probabilities are chosen.

Data

Play-by-play, schedules and betting lines, rosters, injury reports, snap counts and FTN charting (play action, motion, blitzers, pass rushers, interception-worthy throws), all from nflverse through the nflreadpy package. Also from nflverse: NFL Next Gen Stats (time to throw, tight-window throws, rush yards over expected, separation and cushion, stacked boxes) and Pro Football Reference charting (pressures, broken and missed tackles), expected fantasy points and weekly player stats from nflverse, and ESPN QBR. The site rebuilds Thursday morning (full report and the Thursday line), then Saturday and Sunday morning (newer injury statuses for the model). Once a game kicks off, its report is frozen.

Cleaning

  • Plays: regular-season passes and runs only. No kneels, spikes, penalty-only plays or special teams. Sacks and scrambles count as dropbacks.
  • Garbage time removed: efficiency stats use plays with a win probability between 10% and 90%.
  • Neutral script: tendency stats (how often a team throws) use a stricter filter: win probability 20% to 80%, a one-score game, and not the last 2 minutes of a half.
  • Recency: each week's plays are weighted with a 6-week half-life, so recent form counts more.

Metrics

EPA (expected points added)
The change in expected points from before a play to after it, based on down, distance, field position and time. +0.10 EPA per play is good, 0 is average.
Success rate
Share of plays with positive EPA.
Explosive play rate
Share of plays that are dropbacks of 20+ yards or designed runs of 10+ yards.
Pass rate over expected (PROE)
Actual pass rate minus the nflverse expected pass rate for the same situations. Positive = throws more than a typical team would.
Completion % over expected (CPOE)
Completion rate minus the expected rate given throw depth and situation (nflverse model).
aDOT
Average depth of target: air yards per pass attempt. "Air yards vs. normal" for a defense compares opponents' aDOT against their own season average.
YAC over expected
Yards after catch minus the nflverse expected YAC for that catch.
Stuff rate
Share of designed runs that gain 0 yards or less.
Pressure rate
Share of dropbacks with pressure. When charted pressure data is not available for a season, the site uses QB hits plus sacks instead and labels it "QB hit + sack rate".
Shrunk to league mean
Small samples are pulled toward the league average in proportion to their noise (empirical Bayes, method of moments). A team with 40 plays moves more than a team with 400.
Brier score
Mean squared error of win probabilities. Lower is better. Always guessing 50% scores 0.250.
Factor contributions (SHAP)
How many points each model input pushes the projected margin. The model is linear, so these values are exact and sum to the projection.
Playoff leverage
How much a game moves each team's playoff odds: P(playoffs if win) minus P(playoffs if loss), summed over both teams.

Opponent adjustment

Pass and rush EPA are opponent-adjusted with a play-level ridge regression: each play's EPA = offense effect + defense effect + home field. The ridge penalty equals the noise variance over the signal variance, so the regression is also the shrinkage. Passing ratings use only the projected starting QB's dropbacks.

Cards & keys

Each card template pairs an offensive measure with the same measure allowed by defenses. The edge for a matchup is z(offense) + z(opposing defense), where each z is the shrunk value over the raw league spread, so noisy stats score lower. Tendency cards (play action, blitz, man coverage) scale the edge by how often the choosing side uses it. Run-gap cards pick the best of 7 gaps, so they pay a fixed penalty for that extra choice. The top cards are shown, with at most 2 per phase. The 3 keys to success per team are the largest edges involving that team, one per phase: "EDGE" if it favors the team, "WATCH" if it does not.

The "Who has the edge" summary is written by an LLM from these keys and cards only. Every number it uses is checked against the data. If a check fails, a rule-based summary is shown, and the label says so.

Win-probability model

A ridge regression on the home team's margin, converted to a win probability with a normal distribution: P(win) = Φ(margin / σ). Inputs (home minus away): passing matchup, rushing matchup, starting QB value (EPA per dropback, shrunk toward replacement level), offensive and defensive injury load (players listed Out, Doubtful or Questionable, weighted by recent snap share), rest, and home field. All inputs use only games before the one being predicted. The ridge penalty is picked by leave-one-season-out validation and σ by log loss. The track record is a walk-forward backtest since 2018: each season is predicted by a model trained only on earlier seasons and scored against the closing line.

It is a different lens on the data, not betting advice. The market usually beats it.

Luck adjustments in the model

  • Record luck does not enter the model. It rates teams on play-by-play efficiency, not wins or points, so close-game luck (the LUCK column on the league table) never moves its odds. Playoff seeds still use real records, because that is how the NFL seeds.
  • Fumble-recovery luck: who falls on a loose ball is close to a coin flip. A second version of the ratings gives every fumble play the season's average result for a fumble (pass or run) instead of what happened. Each build fits both versions and keeps the one with the lower leave-one-season-out log loss, so this is only used when it predicts better.
  • Injury luck: "Injuries behind ratings" is the recency-weighted injury load (players listed Out, Doubtful or Questionable, weighted by snap share) each team carried in the games its ratings come from, home minus away. The model learns from past seasons how much a team that played hurt tends to beat its ratings. Future weeks and playoff games are then projected at full health (no current injury load).
  • Limits: players on injured reserve leave the weekly report, so long-term injuries count only while listed, and a projection at full health is wrong for a player who is out for the season. Interception luck is not adjusted yet: the charting that flags interception-worthy throws only goes back to 2022.

Track record

Every finished game is graded with the Brier score and log loss, for the model and for the market (vig-free moneyline, else the spread), on the same games. Ties are left out.

  • Published picks: the last version of each game shown on this site before kickoff, graded against the market number from that same build. This is the honest live record.
  • Season walk-forward: every game this season, where week N is predicted by a model fit only on games before week N. It uses the QB who actually started, so it is slightly optimistic, and the market number is the closing line.
  • Past seasons: the same walk-forward backtest by season, plus a calibration chart (predicted vs. actual win rate in 10-point bins).

About 270 games per season is a small sample. A Brier gap smaller than about 0.01 is mostly noise.

Backfilled weeks

Weeks built after the season started (marked "backfilled") use only data from before that week, the same as a live build, but the line is the closing line and the injury report is the final one for that week. They get recaps like any other week. On the track record page they have their own tile, separate from published picks (which only counts picks shown on the site before kickoff), and they are marked BACKFILL in the picks table.

Next Gen Stats and PFR cards

Weekly Next Gen Stats and PFR rows are combined with the same 6-week recency weights as the play-by-play stats. Player stats need a minimum sample (100 attempts for QBs, 50 carries for RBs, 25 targets for receivers) and are not shrunk. Team stats (stacked boxes, pressure, tackling) are shrunk to the league mean. When PFR pressures are available they replace the "QB hit + sack" proxy for the team pressure card; the play-level proxy is still used for the clean-pocket and four-man-rush cards and for recaps. None of these are opponent-adjusted.

Special teams and fourth downs

  • Special teams EPA per game: expected points added on punts, kickoffs, field goals and extra points, credited to the kicking or receiving side, recency-weighted and shrunk to the league mean.
  • Field goal points over expected: 3 × (field goals made minus expected makes), where the expected make rate comes from nflverse's field goal model when present, otherwise from a logistic fit on kick distance. Not shrunk; kickers need 5+ attempts.
  • Fourth-down aggressiveness: share of 4th-and-1 or 4th-and-2 at or past the team's own 40 where the offense runs a play instead of punting or kicking, with win probability 10-90% and outside the last 2 minutes of each half. Shrunk to the league mean.

Elo

Our own Elo ratings, built only from final scores since 2015 (538-style): K = 20, home field worth 48 Elo points, a margin-of-victory multiplier that is damped for heavy favorites, and one third of each rating pulled back to the average (1505) every offseason. Elo ignores how teams play, so it is an independent check on the model. It is shown next to the model and market and graded on the track record page.

Fantasy tab

Up to six cards per game from nflverse's expected fantasy points model (PPR), weekly player stats and ESPN QBR, ranked by how unusual each finding is: points per game over or under expected, touchdowns over or under expected, expected points per game (opportunity), points allowed by the opposing defense to each position, WOPR (1.5 × target share + 0.7 × air-yard share), QBR, and snap-share trends (offensive snap share in a player's last 3 games minus his earlier games, from PFR snap counts). Expected points come from each player's targets, carries, air yards and field position, so a big gap between actual and expected usually shrinks. Season-to-date averages, not recency-weighted, and points allowed are not adjusted for the offenses a defense faced.

Post-game recaps

After a game, each key to success is checked against the same stat in that game, using plays with a win probability between 10% and 90%. The offense "wins" a key if its in-game value beats the league average for that stat (for pressure and stuff rate, if it is lower). A team wins the key if its side won it. Keys need a minimum number of plays (for example 8 dropbacks for passing EPA); below that they are marked "too few plays". Run-gap, coverage-shell, form and luck keys are not graded. Recaps are added on the next build after nflverse publishes the game's play-by-play, usually the next morning.

League table & team pages

NET = opponent-adjusted offensive EPA per play minus opponent-adjusted defensive EPA per play allowed (all plays, garbage time removed, recency-weighted). Pass and rush ranks use the same adjustment as the cards. TREND compares today's NET rank with the NET rank from plays through 4 weeks earlier. PROJ is the average final record across the 10,000 season simulations. LUCK = actual wins minus Pythagorean expected wins, where expected win % = PF2.37 / (PF2.37 + PA2.37). The weekly chart on team pages shows raw game values, so it does not remove opponent strength.

Projected weeks

Every remaining regular-season game through week 18 gets the full set of cards, built from the same data as this week (so the matchup stats are what each team has done so far). The model uses today's ratings and projected starters, no injury report, and the scheduled rest. The summary is rule-based. Projections are rebuilt on every run, so they improve as the season goes on.

Playoff bracket

Until the regular season ends, seeds come from projected final records: each team's average final win total across 10,000 simulations of the remaining games (real results so far plus the model's ratings for the rest). Division winners are seeds 1-4, then three wild cards. Projected ties are rare; when they happen, and once the season is over and real standings take over, ties are broken by head-to-head (within the tied group), division record (same division only), conference record, point differential, then team name. This is approximate: the NFL's full list has more steps, such as common games and strength of victory. Each round is filled by the model favorite; the higher seed hosts and the Super Bowl is neutral. After week 18, real matchups and results from the schedule replace projections as they are set.

Playoff leverage & standings

The rest of the season is simulated 10,000 times. This week's games use the market spread, and later games use the model's ratings, regressed toward even. Seeding is approximate: division winners, then wild cards by wins, with division wins and then a coin flip as tiebreakers.

Limits

  • Mid-season samples are small. Shrinkage helps, but single-game swings still matter.
  • Charted data (coverage shells, true pressure) is not always available for the current season. Cards that need it are skipped.
  • Injury reports change after Friday. Inactives announced 90 minutes before kickoff are not included.
